## The headline
**A whole animal, in three taps.** Protein Outfitters is the marketplace that connects ranchers, USDA-inspected processors, and the families who eat what they raise — replacing phone tags, spreadsheets, and freezer-paper chaos with a Tesla-style purchase flow.

## The problem
Buying a side of beef directly from a rancher should be the most premium meat experience available. Instead, it looks like this: a Google Sheet, three phone calls to a processor with a six-month wait, a paper cut sheet faxed back and forth, and a cooler-shaped guess about what 220 pounds of beef actually means. The customer gets great meat, but only after surviving the operational pain. The producer gets a check eventually. The processor gets yet another walk-in customer with no paper trail.

Roughly 90% of US households want to buy local meat directly from a ranch. Less than 5% actually do — because the buying process is brutal.

## The solution
Protein Outfitters fixes the buying experience without changing the supply chain. Buyers reserve a quarter, half, or whole animal in three taps. They build a custom cut sheet through an animal-aware UI that knows which cuts are available from a steer vs. a hog vs. a lamb. The first buyer locks in a USDA-inspected processor. We coordinate the drop-off, generate a signed QR code, and confirm pickup. Producers get paid the day after pickup.

## Why now
Three trends converged in the last 36 months: USDA inspection capacity finally reopened post-pandemic backlog, direct-to-consumer meat sales doubled, and Stripe Connect made instant marketplace payments table stakes. The infrastructure to build this didn't exist five years ago.

## Where we operate
Launching in Minnesota, Wisconsin, North Dakota, and South Dakota. Powered by Stittsworth Meats, a fourth-generation family meat operation in Bemidji, MN, that has run direct-to-consumer beef for two decades.

## The moat
- **The cut sheet** — animal-aware, processor-agnostic, gets remembered between purchases. Nobody else has this UI.
- **First-purchaser locks processor** — solves the marketplace's hardest coordination problem without anyone having to phone-tag.
- **Insurance pool** — every purchase contributes a few dollars; if the USDA condemns an animal mid-process, the buyer is made whole. Pulls a category of risk that scares first-time buyers off the market.
- **Hardware track** — Friesla Mobile Processing Units expand processor capacity in regions where fixed plants are months out.
