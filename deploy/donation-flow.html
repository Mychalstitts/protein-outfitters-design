Protein Outfitters Donation Depot

Donate an animal.
Feed a school.

A two-sided depot, the way a clothing-donation depot works. Producers, processors, and buyers donate animals or fractions and walk away with a tax-deductible donation form. Qualified institutions — schools, government feeding programs, food banks, and food shelves — register, get approved, and request the meat we have on hand.

Donate

Drop off, get a form, walk away.

If you have an unsold fraction, an excess animal, or a buyer's share you'd like to donate, we route it through the same processor pipeline as a paid sale. Program funds cover the kill fee. You get a tax-deductible acknowledgment letter.

  • Farmers — donate unsold fractions or whole animals
  • Processors — donate excess inventory or salvageable yield
  • Buyers — donate part of your share at checkout
Receive

Apply, qualify, request.

If you're a qualified feeding program, get on our recipient list once. From then on, you see real-time inventory of available donated meat, request what you need, and we coordinate pickup or delivery from the depot's processor partners.

  • Schools — K-12, child nutrition, and Farm-to-School programs
  • Government — state-run feeding, tribal nations, veterans, corrections
  • Food banks & food shelves — 501(c)(3) and equivalents
01 Donor drops off Farmer, processor, or buyer flips a toggle on a listing or share. The fraction is removed from sale.

03 Institution receives Qualified school / government / food bank requests from inventory. Pickup or delivery is arranged.

About the deduction: For raised livestock, the IRS generally allows a deduction at your tax basis (your accumulated production costs). For most cash-basis farmers those costs were already deducted as expenses, so the deduction is small — but you also avoid recognizing income on a sale you didn't make. Some donations of "wholesome food" qualify for up to 2× basis. Talk to your CPA for your specific situation. Watch for state-level credits in Minnesota.
